Build a preparation plan that holds up under pressure

Good preparation is more than memorising a list of expected questions. Start with a timed baseline, note where you hesitate, and alternate concept study with spoken practice. A strong candidate can explain a project clearly, check assumptions aloud, and stay composed when a follow-up question changes direction.

Use these guides as a roadmap, then practise the ideas in your own words. A weekly record of questions, feedback, and next actions makes progress visible and keeps your plan grounded in evidence.

Keep learning, then practise

After a mock interview, return to the guide that addresses your weakest dimension and apply one technique in your next attempt. The most useful target is specific: reduce filler words, state assumptions before coding, quantify an outcome, or finish with a clear result.

PlacementDo resources are educational and cannot predict a hiring decision. Verify company-specific details against the employer’s current job description and official assessment instructions, and ask a mentor for context when you need a second perspective.
